## How historians explain Plassey
- **Sushil Chaudhury:** the British engineered it. Proceedings of the Indian History Congress 1998, excerpted in the Daily Star, 21 Jun 2020 [FT]: "It was the British who were too anxious to bring about the revolution." He rejects both the claim of a pre-1757 economic crisis and the communal-split thesis (Itinerario 15(2), 1991) [EX]. Banglapedia (Mohsin/Chaudhury): "about two-thirds of the nawab's army under Mir Jafar, Yar Latif and Ray Durlabh Ram merely stood by and watched." https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Palashi,_The_Battle_of
- **Marshall and Bayly:** the Indian factions drew the British in. Marshall, East Indian Fortunes (1976), p. 8 [FT]: "Siraj-ud-daula's position was a weak one... willing... to use Clive's army as their instrument." Bayly, Indian Society and the Making of the British Empire (1988), pp. 46–50 [FT]: "a fortuitous revolution."
- **Bandyopadhyay,** From Plassey to Partition (2004) [FT]: whether the plot was Indian-born or English-hatched is a "futile polemical" debate; "What is important is the fact that there was a collusion."
- **Brijen K. Gupta,** Sirajuddaullah and the East India Company (1962): private trade is central. His "The Black Hole Incident," JAS 19 (1959) [EX]: Sarkar's view that "probably not more than sixty were confined." The figures 64 went in / 21 survived are unverified [SN].
- **Rajat Kanta Ray,** Palashir Sharajantra o Sekaler Samaj (1994): his argument is unverified.
- **Partha Chatterjee,** Daily Star, 24 Jun 2018 [FT]: "the colonial myth of the Black Hole was actually authored by Thomas Macaulay in 1840." Maitra's 1897 book made Siraj a tragic hero; Girish Ghosh's play was banned; Sachin Sengupta's 1938 play had music by Nazrul.
- **Irfan Habib,** MAS 1985 [FT]: the purpose of 1757 was "to enable Britain to obtain Indian goods without any export of treasure in return."
- **Dalrymple,** Guardian, 4 Mar 2015 [FT]: "owed more to treachery, forged contracts, bankers and bribes than military prowess." Critics: Tirthankar Roy, TLS 2019 (paywalled); Swarajya review [FT].
- **Macaulay,** "Lord Clive" (1840) [FT]: the imperial "oriental despot" framing, and 146 / 23 for the Black Hole. Panikkar (1947), p. 244: Holwell "was a known liar."
- **Communal framings:**
  - Panikkar (1947), pp. 244–5: "Hindu merchants".
  - Banglapedia's Jagat Sheth entry: "the money of Jagat Sheth together with the sword of the English brought about the fall of Muslim power."
  - BJP candidate Amrita Roy (2024): Krishnachandra sided with Clive "to protect the Hindu Sanatana Dharma."
  - The record shows mixed sides on both sides: Mir Madan, Mohanlal and Naba Singh Hazari fought.
  - The Jagat Seths were Oswal **Jains**, not "Hindu bankers" (Sahapedia).

## Myths vs record
1. **"3,000 beat 50,000."** The numbers are roughly right, but two-thirds of the Nawab's army never fought. Bandyopadhyay: "hardly more than a skirmish."
2. **"A transaction, not a battle."** This is Panikkar, Asia and Western Dominance (1953), about p. 100 [FT]: "Plassey ... was a transaction, not a battle, a transaction by which the compradors of Bengal, led by Jagat Seth, sold the nawab to the East India Company."
3. **"Mir Jafar" as a word for traitor.** It's used both ways in Bengal politics. Iqbal's couplet: verify the exact Persian.
4. **Siraj as debauchee or as patriot.** Both images were built later. Banglapedia: "in his inexperience, he tried to deal with all his opponents at the same time."
5. **The Black Hole.** It happened, but with fewer victims than Holwell claimed. "Pure fiction" is wrong.
6. **"Rain won it."** John Wood's account, written within two weeks (Furber & Glamann, JAS 1960) [EX], mentions only a fifteen-minute shower.
7. **"India conquered in 1757."** No. Plassey plus Buxar (1764) made Bengal theirs; the conquest of India ran 1757–1818.
8. **Siraj's "last words."** No reliable quotation found.
9. **Siraj's killing.** Muhammadi Beg killed him on Miran's orders (Banglapedia), on 2 or 3 July 1757. Macaulay: "the English bore no part."
10. **Lutfunnisa at Khushbagh.** This rests on local tradition.
11. **"Jagat Seth richer than the Bank of England."** Banglapedia compares function, not wealth. "$1 trillion" figures are baseless.
12. **Clive's jagir.** About £27,000 a year (Marshall 1976, pp. 165–6). Presents in 1757 came to £1,238,575, per the Commons committee.
13. **"Astonished at my own moderation."** Said in the 1772 Select Committee examination. Verify the exact wording in its First Report.

## Commemoration
- The Palashi memorial stone dates from 1883; the present structure is from Curzon's time (nadia.gov.in).
- At the 250th anniversary in 2007, a Siraj bust was placed before the British memorial. Debabrata Biswas: "Both monuments will remain... We need both" (BBC, 29 Jun 2007).
- Khushbagh holds Siraj, Lutfunnisa and Alivardi. Namak Haram Deorhi is in Murshidabad. In 2026, Mir Jafar's descendants were struck off the voter rolls (Deccan Chronicle, 1 Apr 2026).
