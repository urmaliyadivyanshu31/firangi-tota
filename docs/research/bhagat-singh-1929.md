# Bhagat Singh and the HSRA, Oct 1928 – Mar 1931: research dossier

Prepared for the game team. Every fact carries a source and a confidence label: **solid** (primary record or several independent good sources agree), **disputed** (sources disagree; range given), or **unverified** (often repeated, but I found no good source). Weekdays were computed from the calendar, not copied from secondary sources.

---

## 1. Summary

On 30 October 1928, police broke up a protest in Lahore against the Simon Commission with a lathi charge, and Lala Lajpat Rai was among those beaten. He died on 17 November. A small, mostly student underground group had renamed itself the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA) only weeks earlier. It decided to kill the police chief, J.A. Scott. On 17 December its lookout signalled at the wrong man, and the HSRA shot John Poyntz Saunders, a 21-year-old probationer. Head Constable Chanan Singh, who chased them, was shot dead too. Overnight, Lahore's walls carried notices that claimed the act, signed "Balraj, Commander-in-Chief". Bhagat Singh then cut his hair, shaved, dressed as a Westernised young official and left Lahore by first class through a police cordon. Durgawati Devi rode with him posing as his wife, carrying her small son, and Rajguru played their servant. Four months later, on 8 April 1929, Bhagat Singh and B.K. Dutt threw two bombs, designed (they said) not to kill, into the Central Legislative Assembly in Delhi. They showered pink leaflets ("It takes a loud voice to make the deaf hear") and waited to be arrested. The pistol on Bhagat Singh matched a cartridge case from the Saunders shooting. One week later, a blacksmith's tip and a sulphur-stained drain led police to the Lahore bomb factory. Seven comrades turned approver. A special tribunal set up by ordinance sentenced Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru to death. They were hanged on the evening of 23 March 1931, ahead of the date the public expected. That same morning Gandhi had sent the Viceroy a final written appeal, which was refused. The drama works for a game because the whole story turns on true and false messages: notices, aliases, disguises, recognition tokens, approvers, intercepted letters and a forensic match.

### 2c. Wider arc (brief)

- **8–9 Sep 1928 (Sat–Sun):** At a Feroz Shah Kotla meeting in Delhi, the HRA becomes the HSRA (solid: Chaman Lal, Reader intro; Manmathnath Gupta 1972 misdates it to 8 Dec).
- **30 Oct 1928 (Tue):** the Simon Commission protest in Lahore.
- **17 Nov 1928 (Sat):** Lajpat Rai dies.
- **8 Apr 1929:** the Assembly action.
- **7 May – 12 Jun 1929:** Assembly Bomb Case. The statement is read by Asaf Ali on 6 Jun, and the pair are sentenced to transportation for life on 12 Jun. Appeal dismissed 13 Jan 1930 by Forde and Addison JJ.
- **15 Jun 1929:** Bhagat Singh and Dutt begin a hunger strike.
- **10 Jul 1929:** the Lahore Conspiracy Case opens before Special Magistrate Rai Sahib Pt. Sri Kishen. Bhagat Singh is brought in on a stretcher.
- **13 Jul 1929:** other accused join the strike.
- **13 Sep 1929 (Fri):** Jatin Das dies after 63 days.
- **30 Sep 1929:** Rajguru is arrested in a Poona garage.
- **21 Oct 1929:** Prem Dutt throws a slipper at approver Jai Gopal. All accused are then handcuffed on both hands.
- **23 Dec 1929:** a bomb goes off under the Viceroy's special train near Delhi (Bhagwati Charan and Yashpal). Gandhi writes "The Cult of the Bomb"; the HSRA replies with "The Philosophy of the Bomb" (Jan 1930).
- **1 May 1930:** Ordinance No. III of 1930 is signed "Simla, IRWIN".
- **5 May 1930:** the Tribunal opens.
- **12 May 1930:** the accused are beaten in court. Justice Agha Haider dissociates himself from the order.
- **28 May 1930:** Bhagwati Charan dies testing a bomb near the Ravi.
- **21 Jun 1930:** the Tribunal is reconstituted without Agha Haider.
- **7 Oct 1930 (Tue):** the judgment.
- **8 Oct 1930:** Durga Devi's Lamington Road shooting in Bombay.
- **11 Feb 1931:** the Privy Council dismisses the appeal.
- **27 Feb 1931 (Fri):** Azad dies at Alfred Park, Allahabad.
- **5 Mar 1931:** the Gandhi–Irwin Pact.
- **19 Mar (Thu) and 21 Mar (Sat) 1931:** Gandhi raises the case with Irwin.
- **23 Mar 1931 (Mon):** at 3 p.m. the High Court dismisses the last petitions; the three are hanged that evening.
- **24 Mar 1931:** remains are recovered and cremated by the Ravi.
- **29 Mar 1931:** the Karachi Congress resolution.

## 3. Persona options

**A. The "post box" (best fit).** A Lahore student or sympathiser who receives and forwards notes for absconders. This role is real: Maclean, citing Comrade Ram Chandra, records that Durga Devi acted as a "post box", receiving mail for absconding revolutionaries, in 1929. Notes claim to come from "Panditji", "Balraj", "Villager" (Sukhdev) or "M" (Rajguru). The forger is a CID plant. This is also real: in January 1929 a Naujawan Bharat Sabha member, Babu Singh, offered SP Abdul Aziz clues in return for a Rs 1,000 reward. The player checks notes against party rules that are on record:
- notices are signed "Balraj, Commander-in-Chief";
- recognition is by token (Jai Gopal testified that Sukhdev told him to hand over a zinc handbag only "to the person who showed the said keys");
- the party code was never to ask about a comrade's past (Ajoy Ghosh, 1945).

Works in either window.

**B. A clerk in the Lahore CID's political branch, secretly sympathetic.** Bhagat Singh's own letter is addressed to "The Superintendent (Special Duty), C.I.D. (Political Branch), Lahore", so the office is real. Chaman Lal reports that CID files suspected police officers of leaking to the Hindustan Times. The player sees the government side's real traffic: tip-offs, the blacksmith's report, identification parades and the Viceroy's telegrams. The task is to pass true warnings out and spot traps planted to catch leakers. Strongest in the April window.

**C. A night-desk reporter, Delhi or Lahore, 8–15 April 1929.** Real models: Bhikshu Chaman Lal of the Hindustan Times picked up the leaflet in the Assembly, and Durga Das telegraphed the story to London. Statements, telegrams and photos arrive, and the player must decide what to print. Genuine HSRA items share fixed features: pink paper, a printed heading "The Hindustan Socialist Republican Army", the signature "Balraj" and the slogan "Long Live Revolution". The forger's items carry wrong names or dates, or police vocabulary such as "outrage" and "miscreants". The newsroom offers great sound and texture: teleprinter, telegraph boys, the Urdu and English papers.

## 5. Real messages: seed texts for the "genuine" pile

Quoted from the editions named. Where a text exists in more than one wording, use one edition consistently.

1. **HSRA notice, 18 Dec 1928** (shahidbhagatsingh.org transcription on Marxists.org): "HINDUSTAN SOCIALIST REPUBLICAN ARMY / NOTICE / J.P. Sunders is dead; Lala Lajpat Rai is avenged … Beware, Ye Tyrants; Beware … Sorry for the death of a man. But in this man has died the representative of an institution which is so cruel, lowly and so base that it must be abolished … 'Long Live The Revolution'! Sd/- Balraj, Dated 18th December, 1928."
   - The misspelling "Sunders" is in that transcription. Noorani and the Reader give a different English version that opens "'Bureaucracy Beware' – With the death of J.P. Saunders the assassination of Lala Lajpat Rai has been avenged" and includes "We are sorry to have killed a man." Sanyal (1931) and the Tribunal quote the legend as "Saunders is dead, Lalaji is avenged".
   - **Design warning:** genuine copies vary, so don't use the headline wording as a fake-detector.

2. **Second notice, 23 Dec 1928** (Reader): "The Hindustan Socialist Republican Army / Notice / No more secrets No more Special Guess / About / The incident of 17 December / JP Saunders is dead! / Lala Lajpat Rai is avenged!! / Under the rules and regulations of the H.S.R.A. (Rule 10 B.R.C) it is hereby notified that it was retaliatory action of none but a direct political nature … everybody is hereby requested to abstain from offering any sort of assistance to our enemy the police in finding out our clues. Anybody acting contrary will be severely dealt with. Long Live Revolution / Balraj / Commander-in-chief."

3. **Assembly leaflet, 8 Apr 1929** (Marxists.org): "THE HINDUSTAN SOCIALIST REPUBLICAN ARMY (NOTICE) – It takes a loud voice to make the deaf hear, with these immortal words uttered on a similar occasion by Valiant [Vaillant], a French anarchist martyr, do we strongly justify this action of ours … the Government is thrusting upon us new repressive measures like the Public Safety and the Trade Disputes Bill, while reserving the Press Sedition Bill for the next session … Let the representatives of the people return to their constituencies and prepare the masses for the coming revolution … it is easy to kill individuals but you cannot kill the ideas … 'Long Live the Revolution.' Signed, Balraj, Commander-in-Chief."

4. **Letter to Sukhdev, 5 Apr 1929:** "Dear Brother, By the time you receive this letter I will be gone, going to a far off destination … I am not weak, not weaker than anyone amongst us, brother … Yours, B. S." It was recovered from Sukhdev on arrest and used as an exhibit.

5. **Joint statement, read by Asaf Ali, 6 Jun 1929:**
   - "the evidence of Sergeant Terry regarding the seizure of the pistol from one of us is a deliberate falsehood, for neither of us had the pistol at the time we gave ourselves up."
   - "Our sole purpose was 'to make the deaf hear' … We have only hoisted the 'danger-signal'."
   - The bombs caused "slight damage to an empty bench and some slight abrasions in less than half a dozen cases."
   - "we could have ambushed Sir John Simon … who was sitting in the President's gallery."
   - On revolution: "'Revolution' does not necessarily involve sanguinary strife nor is there any place in it for individual vendetta. It is not the cult of the bomb and the pistol."

6. **Letter to the CID, undated, annotated 31 May 1929** (Marxists.org, transcribed from the original): "The Superintendent (Special Duty) / C.I.D. (Political Branch) / Lahore / Dear Sir, I will feel much obliged if you will kindly allow me to have an interview with my father, as I have got some very important instructions to give him for my defense counsel in connection with the Delhi case … Hoping to be favoured, Yours Etc. Bhagat Singh." This is the ideal template for formatting real mail to the CID.

7. **To the Editor, Modern Review** (Tribune, 24 Dec 1929), signed by Bhagat Singh and B.K. Dutt: "We are not the originators of this cry. The same cry had been used in Russian revolutionary movement … when we shout 'Long Live Jatin Das', we cannot and do not mean thereby that Das should physically be alive."

8. **Letter to his father, 4 Oct 1930:** "I feel as though I have been stabbed at the back … Let me say, father, you have failed … My life is not so precious, at least to me, as you may probably think it to be. It is not at all worth buying at the cost of my principles."

9. **"Why I am an Atheist"**, written 5–6 Oct 1930 and first printed in *The People*, Lahore, 27 Sep 1931:
   - "I had begun to preserve the unshorn and unclipped long hair but I could never believe in the mythology and doctrines of Sikhism or any other religion."
   - "Use of force justifiable when resorted to as a matter of terrible necessity: non-violence as policy indispensable for all mass movements."
   - "It is not my vanity, my friends. It is my mode of thinking that has made me an atheist."

10. **"To Young Political Workers", 2 Feb 1931:** "Apparently I have acted like a terrorist. But I am not a terrorist … Let me announce with all the strength at my command, that I am not a terrorist and I never was, except perhaps in the beginning of my revolutionary career."

11. **Petition to the Punjab Governor** (undated; Chaman Lal places it three days before the execution): "according to the verdict of your court we had waged war and were therefore war prisoners. And we claim to be treated as such, i.e., we claim to be shot dead instead of to be hanged … order the military department to send its detachment to perform our execution."

12. **Last letter to comrades, 22 Mar 1931** (Chaman Lal's English translation): "It is natural that the desire to live should be in me as well, I don't want to hide it … My name has become a symbol of Hindustani revolution … no temptation to escape the noose has ever come to me."

13. **Gandhi to Irwin, 23 Mar 1931**, from "1 Daryaganj, Delhi" (CWMG C.W. 9343): "It seems cruel to inflict this letter on you, but the interest of peace demands a final appeal … Popular opinion rightly or wrongly demands commutation … Execution is an irretrievable act. If you think there is the slightest chance of error of judgment, I would urge you to suspend for further review an act that is beyond recall … 'Charity never faileth.'"

14. **The government side (genuine; useful as "official" mail):**
    - Irwin, 29 Dec 1928: the investigation "is not making much progress".
    - Irwin to the Secretary of State, 19 Apr 1929: "leaflets … typed on pink paper with printed heading which is identical in all respects with that used in threatening manuscript notices pasted up in Lahore … preliminary microscopic examination of automatic pistol found with him favours view that it was one of weapons used."
    - Irwin's note, 19 Mar 1931: "I told him I had considered the case with most anxious care, but could find no grounds on which I could justify to my conscience commuting the sentence."
    - Irwin's reply to the 23 Mar letter: "I cannot see my way to feel that it would be right to take the action you request."

15. **Headlines.** Tribune, 10 Apr 1929: see §2a. Tribune, 25 Mar 1931: "BHAGAT SINGH, RAJGURU AND SUKHDEV EXECUTED" / "No Last Interview with Relations" / "Shouts Emerge From Jail" / "Dead Bodies Secretly Disposed of" (per K.C. Yadav, via Tribune 2005).

16. **Court song.** On 4 Oct 1929 the accused sang "Bharat na reh sakega hargiz ghulam khana" ("India will never remain a house of slaves"; Noorani).

## 6. Real deception

**Disguise and cover**
- **The escape disguise:** shaved and haircut, Western clothes, a "young Government Official" with official-name labels on his trunks (Sanyal, 1931). Rajguru played the orderly with a tiffin carrier; Durga Devi and her child completed the picture of a family; both men were armed (Sanyal).
- **Azad's cover:** a panda escorting women pilgrims.
- **At the scene:** Bhagat Singh swapped his cap for Jai Gopal's *lungi* (turban cloth) but could not tie it and dropped it. A head constable found it. Questioned by DSP Morris, Jai Gopal "pretended to be a student reading a book" (Tribunal).
- **At the Assembly:** Western dress, and visitors' passes obtained through a member. Sanyal says they went in for 3–4 days with "a fully charged bomb in one pocket and a loaded revolver in another". Jaidev Kapoor left minutes before and burned the passes. Bhagat Singh gave him a watch and swapped his new shoes for Kapoor's old ones (Chaman Lal).
- **Front businesses:** "Dr B.S. Nigam" (Gaya Prasad) ran dispensaries as fronts, at Ferozepur in Aug 1928 and Saharanpur in 1929. At Agra, iron bomb shells were described to the blacksmith as parts of a "gas machine" (Noorani).

**HSRA tradecraft**
- Party names (see §4, item 15).
- Recognition tokens: the "two keys" for the zinc handbag (Jai Gopal).
- Never ask about a comrade's past (Ajoy Ghosh).
- Notices signed "Balraj" so the real commander stayed anonymous (Yashpal).
- Pre-written posters naming Scott were altered to say Saunders (Noorani: "He had changed the victim's name"; also Nayar).
- Breaking the journey at Lucknow (Maclean).

**Police methods**
- **Informer:** Babu Singh, Jan 1929. Rs 1,000 was drawn from the treasury for his reward and held by the SP; he "shut up" after a while (Abdul Aziz's testimony).
- **Arrests and searches:** 16 suspects arrested within days, including Vohra and Naujawan Bharat Sabha members. Police watched railway stations, and "constables who knew him were posted at big junctions" (Sanyal).
- **The blacksmith tip chain:** ASI Ghulam Sarwar → blacksmith Ghulam Rasool on Brandreth Road → the landlord's register → a private watcher → sulphur on the parnala → a 7 a.m. phone tip.
- **Forensics:** handwriting on the posters, the pink paper and printed heading, the microscopic pistol match and Churchill's cartridge evidence.
- **Photographs:** negatives found at Saharanpur were identified by Ramnath at a parade.
- **Interrogation:** Gupta reports that Dutt was given a soft talk by Tasadduq, a police interrogator. Dutt's clothes were then swapped overnight so the originals could go for forensic examination.
- **Approvers turned by pardon:** see §12.
- **Intercepted letters:** an October 1930 Sukhdev letter was seized by prison guards (NAI, Home Political 135/1930, cited by Maclean).

**Contested deception**
- Sukhdev's statements to police: Vohra claimed in a 1981 letter that he confessed because he was told Sukhdev "had already given away all their secrets". Sukhdev's own marginal remarks are published in Waraich's volumes, which I did not read. Treat as disputed.
- Azad's betrayal at Alfred Park: an informer is often named (Virbhadra Tiwari), but the claim is unverified. Gupta names a man called Dalchand who identified Azad to police.

## 7. Tempting wrong variants (DESIGN INPUT: invented near-misses, not history)

**Name pairs that are both real but different people** (good hard puzzles):
- Hans Raj **Vohra** (approver) vs Bhagwati Charan **Vohra** (HSRA ideologue). Not related, as far as I found.
- **Ajoy** Ghosh (comrade, acquitted) vs **Phanindra Nath** Ghosh (approver).
- **Jatindra Nath Das** (died on hunger strike) vs **Jatindra Nath Sanyal** (co-accused, acquitted, author of the 1931 biography) vs **Sachindra Nath Sanyal** (HRA founder, Kakori convict).
- **Kartar Singh** (pen name on "The Philosophy of the Bomb") vs **Kartar Singh Sarabha** (Ghadar martyr, hanged 1915).
- **Kishori Lal** (accused) vs **Kishan Singh** (Bhagat Singh's father).

**Simple misspellings** (easy fakes): "Chanan" → "Charan" Singh; "Rajguru alias M" → "alias R"; "Villager" → "Farmer"; "Panditji" → "Babuji"; "Balraj" → "Baldev" or "Balram"; "Parbhat" → "Prakash"; "Batukeshwar Dutt" → "Das". Avoid relying on "Saunders/Sunders" or "Fearn/Fern": genuine sources vary.

**Neighbouring places:**
- Lahore: Mozang House → "Mazang Road"; Kirpa Ram Street → "Kirpa Narain Street"; Kashmir Buildings, McLeod Road → "on the Mall"; Brandreth Road blacksmith → "Nisbet Road"; Mayo Hospital → "Ganga Ram Hospital"; DAV College → "Government College"; Mori Gate meeting → "Bhati Gate".
- Delhi: Kashmere Gate studio → "Ajmeri Gate"; Qudsia Garden → "Roshanara Garden"; Sita Ram Bazar → "Sadar Bazar".
- Elsewhere: Saharanpur → Moradabad; Hing ki Mandi (Agra) → "Kinari Bazar".

**Off-by-a-bit facts:**
- Saunders shot 17 Dec → 15 Dec. Gupta 1972 really prints this error, so a forger copying a careless book is plausible.
- The HSRA founded 8–9 Sep → "8 Dec" (another real Gupta error).
- Jatin Das 63 days → 61 or 64.
- Ordinance No. III → No. II.
- House No. 69 → 96; rent Rs 13 → Rs 31.
- Car No. 6728 → 6278.
- Assembly bombs "about 12:30" → "11 a.m., at the opening of the sitting".
- Executed 23 Mar → "24 Mar", the date the press expected. Perfect trap: a genuine-looking letter on 22 Mar could still mention "24th".

**Weekday traps:**
- 17 Dec 1928 and 8 Apr 1929 were Mondays; 20 Dec 1928 was a Thursday.
- 23 Mar 1931 was a Monday, Gandhi's silence day. A "Gandhi said today, Monday the 23rd" message is suspect; he wrote that day.

**Anachronisms a forger might slip in:**
- "Jai Hind" (popularised in the 1940s).
- "Netaji" for Bose (a 1940s usage).
- "Shaheed-e-Azam" before 1931.
- "Gandhi–Irwin Pact" before 5 Mar 1931; "Purna Swaraj" before Dec 1929.
- "Pakistan" (the word was coined in 1933).
- "Hussainiwala memorial".
- Calling the 8 Apr 1929 building "Parliament House" (then the Council House).

**Habits that give away the police (design suggestions):**
- **Revolutionary:** notices headed "Notice"; signed "Balraj, Commander-in-Chief"; English with Hindustani slogans; pink paper with printed heading; ends "Long Live Revolution". Comrades use party names and never ask about the past. They call the act an "action".
- **Police and government:** "the accused", "absconder", "culprits", "miscreants" (Irwin's word), "outrage" (the official name was "Assembly Bomb Outrage"); full real names; offers of reward money; asking where someone lives or comes from; a typed reference number.

## 8. Myths vs facts

| Popular claim | What the record supports |
|---|---|
| "Gandhi did nothing to save him." | The record shows he raised it more than once. On 18 Feb 1931, Irwin's note says Gandhi "did not plead for commutation" but "did ask for postponement"; Mahadev Desai's diary has Gandhi asking Irwin to "suspend Bhagat Singh's execution". He raised it again on 19 Mar, on Sat 21 Mar (his "submission of Saturday"), and in the letter of 23 Mar. He also sent Sapru, Jayakar and Sastri (Anil Nauriya, cited by V.N. Datta). The record equally shows he did not make commutation a condition of the pact ("to threaten withdrawal would be a breach of faith", Karachi). Irwin's and Emerson's notes describe his pleas as mild. **Readings:** A.G. Noorani, Manmathnath Gupta and Yashpal say he did too little. V.N. Datta and Anil Nauriya say he tried hard. Subhas Bose, quoted in *Mainstream*, said Gandhi "did try his very best" while wanting a harder line. Present the documents; don't give a verdict. |
| "Hanged a day early to avoid crowds." | Partly true. The date publicly expected was 24 Mar (Irwin's own note). The hanging took place on the evening of 23 Mar, without a last family interview (Tribune headlines). "To avoid crowds" is an inference widely held by contemporaries and historians. I found no official statement of the motive. |
| "He was hanged at 7:33 p.m. sharp." | Time is disputed: 7:00 (Chaman Lal, citing notices displayed in 2018), 7:15 (Noorani), 7:30 or 7:33 (popular), and "9 pm" (PTI's reading of the jail certificate). Use "evening". |
| "Their bodies were cut up." | The claim comes from family and later accounts (Reader intro; Satwinder Singh Jas). The authorities said rites were performed. Contemporaries agree the bodies were secretly taken to the Sutlej near Ferozepur, burned at night, and partly burnt remains recovered by people who cremated them by the Ravi on 24 Mar. **Avoid gore**: show "secret, hurried, at night". |
| "He was hanged at Hussainiwala." | He was hanged at Lahore Central Jail; the site is now near Shadman Chowk, Lahore. Hussainiwala, near Ferozepur, is the cremation site. |
| The yellow (basanti) turban | No authentic photo shows one. Chaman Lal: "only four original photographs"; "Bhagat Singh never wore any Basanti or Kesari turban." The Tribune of 18 Apr 1929 printed a turban photo. |
| The felt hat | It is genuine, from Ramnath's April 1929 studio portrait, which Maclean shows was made as propaganda. But per Chaman Lal the hat was bought in Calcutta in December 1928. So "the felt hat at Lahore station" is uncertain; sources only say "Western dress". |
| "Mera Rang De Basanti Chola" was his song | No contemporary record says he wrote it. It is usually credited to the Kakori prisoners' circle (Ram Prasad Bismil tradition, 1927). The film versions are modern compositions. The documented court song is "Bharat na reh sakega hargiz ghulam khana". |
| He coined "Inquilab Zindabad" | His own words: "We are not the originators of this cry" (letter to Modern Review). It is commonly credited to Hasrat Mohani (1921). |
| Bhagat Singh threw both bombs | Disputed. Dutt told Manmathnath Gupta that each threw one. A relative, citing Asaf Ali, says Bhagat Singh threw both. |
| They fired pistols in the Assembly | Reported by the Tribune on 10 Apr 1929. The accused denied having a pistol when they surrendered, and Sanyal says they laid their revolvers down. |
| Saunders beat Lajpat Rai | Disputed. Nehru wrote of "a young English police officer". The HSRA notice blamed Saunders, but the planned target was Scott. Nehru on the death: doctors thought the injury "hastened the end". |
| "Named in the FIR" | The FIR named unidentified assailants (Times of India, 2014). Identification came months later. |

## 9. Sensory world

**Lahore streets, December.** Winter cold, early-morning mist. Tongas and bicycles everywhere; the getaway was on bicycles, one grabbed from a cycle shop. A red motorcycle and a motor car (Abdullah's, which took Saunders to hospital). Walled-city gates: Mori, Bhati, Roshnai. The Ravi to the north. Posters pasted at night and read by crowds in the morning.

**Railway.** Lahore's fortress-like station; first-class compartments; trunks with name labels; a tiffin carrier; porters; plainclothes CID at the junctions; the change of trains at Lucknow; the telegraph office.

**The Assembly.** The circular Council House with wooden desks and benches. A tense debate, then "deafening noise" and "dense smoke" (Sanyal). Pink sheets drifting down. Members fleeing; Sanyal mocks those who hid in the lavatories. Slogans in English and Hindustani. Sergeants' boots. A pistol laid on a side table.

**Courts and prison.** Lahore Central Jail and Borstal Jail. Handcuffs on one hand, later both. Daily singing and slogans in court ("noise and confusion", Tribune). Forced feeding during the hunger strike. Lawyers in gowns; an interpreter (Kalandar Ali Khan). On the last evening, "shouts emerge from jail" as the hanging began.

**On a desk.**
- **Revolutionary:** pink paper with a printed heading, a typewriter, the hat photo, a zinc handbag and two keys, notebooks of formulae, chemical tins and sulphur stains, a Mauser and a .32 Colt automatic.
- **CID:** a register, a telephone, identification-parade memoranda, a Rs 1,000 envelope, telegram forms ("Viceroy to Secretary of State").

**Scripts.** English for HSRA notices and court statements. Urdu for Lahore papers (*Bande Mataram*, *Zamindar*, *Milap*). Gurmukhi for *Kirti* and *Akali*. Hindi in Devanagari for *Pratap*, *Chand* and *Arjun*. Bengali in Calcutta. Money in rupees, annas and pice.

**Clothes.** Khaddar for Congress workers. Bhagat Singh in Western suit and felt hat. Chaman Lal says khaki shirt and shorts at the Assembly; Sanyal says "European costume", so this is disputed. Azad in a ramnami shawl as a panda. Police in khaki with turbans or helmets.

**Sounds.** Tonga bells and hooves, cycle bells, steam whistles, a telegraph key clicking, crowds chanting "Inquilab Zindabad", a court gavel, iron gates and a hunger striker's silence.

## 10. Sensitivity notes and the range of views

Let his documented words speak. Use the texts in §5. Where groups claim him, give each claim one line with who holds it.

- **Left and Marxist** (Chaman Lal, Bipan Chandra's NBT introduction to "Why I am an Atheist", the CPI tradition via Ajoy Ghosh's 1945 pamphlet): a socialist and atheist moving towards mass politics. Evidence: the "dictatorship of the proletariat" passage in the 1929 statement, "Why I am an Atheist", and "I am not a terrorist".
- **Hindu right** (e.g., Organiser, 2011: "Bhagat Singh was never a Communist"): emphasises patriotism, his Arya Samaji upbringing (his own words: he recited the Gayatri Mantra "for hours"), and Malaviya's support. It also notes the Communist paper Workers' Weekly criticised the HSRA in Nov 1930; that quote comes via Organiser and I could not see the original.
- **Sikh and Punjabi groups:** a Sikh family; links to Ghadar and the *Kirti* and *Akali* papers; he kept Kartar Singh Sarabha's photo in his pocket (Chaman Lal). Some cite Bhai Randhir Singh's jail letters, which say that on 4 Oct 1930 Bhagat Singh agreed to keep his hair. Editors of "Why I am an Atheist" say the essay (5–6 Oct 1930) answered Randhir Singh's charge of vanity. Present both as claims.
- **Congress:** Gandhi's statement of 24 Mar 1931: "his daring was unequalled … The deliverance of this country must not be through murder". Gandhi drafted the Karachi resolution. Nehru: "magnificent courage and sacrifice".
- **AAP and the Punjab government** today use him heavily as a symbol, for example in the 2022 row over a yellow-turban portrait in the Chief Minister's office.
- **Pakistani Punjab:** the Bhagat Singh Memorial Foundation (Imtiaz Rashid Qureshi) has petitioned Lahore courts to rename Shadman Chowk and to reopen the case. The Lahore administration cited an objection calling him "a criminal", and a petition was dismissed. Reports differ on dates.
- **The Gandhi question:** present the dated documents (§8) side by side. Let the player read Irwin's note and Gandhi's letter.
- **Violence:** two men were killed, and Chanan Singh was an Indian head constable doing his job. The game can stand with the revolutionaries without mocking the dead. The HSRA's own notice began "Sorry for the death of a man."

## 12. Who sided with the British, with receipts

**Read first: fairness rules for this section.**
1. People of every community served on both sides. Collaborators included Hindu approvers (Jai Gopal, Hans Raj Vohra), a Bengali approver (Phanindra Ghosh), British, Muslim, Sikh and Hindu officials and witnesses. Defenders included Justice Agha Haider, who was removed for questioning witnesses closely; Jinnah; Maulana Zafar Ali Khan; and the Sikh, Hindu and Muslim speakers at Lahore's protest meeting. **Mock roles and choices, never a community.** Don't show names to imply religion.
2. **Don't mock the dead or the coerced.** Saunders and Chanan Singh were killed. Ordinary witnesses such as the photographer Ramnath and cycle-shop men were summoned by the police.
3. Each entry gives the documented fact and a short line, with the person's own explanation where one exists.

### Approvers (5 testified; 2 retracted)

- **Jai Gopal.**
  - **Role:** Sukhdev recruited him from the National School in 1926. He gave the signal that pointed the shooters at the wrong man.
  - **Arrest and pardon:** arrested 15 Apr 1929 at Kashmir Buildings; pardoned within days.
  - **What he gave away:** he described the Mozang House meeting where "Panditji proposed the murder of Scott". He identified the pistol and revolver "used by Bhagat Singh and Rajguru alias 'M'" (Noorani, ch. "Magisterial Farce"). He also described the planned Punjab National Bank dacoity and the canal-bridge sighting of Scott.
  - **In court:** on 21 Oct 1929, Prem Dutt "threw a slipper at the approver, Jai Gopal". At one identification parade Jai Gopal "wept copiously and embraced the accused" (Noorani).
  - **Afterwards:** not found. His own explanation: not found.

- **Hans Raj Vohra.**
  - **Background:** Forman Christian College student; worked at the *Bande Mataram* office. Arrested 17 Dec 1928 and bailed on 3 Jan 1929 "without having made any statement" (Tribunal).
  - **What he gave away:** re-arrested May 1929 and pardoned. He gave evidence centred on Punjab and on Bhagat Singh. Kuldip Nayar calls it "crucial in the death sentence" (*The Martyr*, 2000, via Wikipedia).
  - **Afterwards** (Wikipedia, summarising Vaidik): the Punjab government sponsored him at the London School of Economics. He worked for the *Civil and Military Gazette*, then from 1958 was Washington correspondent of the *Times of India*. He died in Maryland in 1985.
  - **His explanation** (1981 letter, via Aparna Vaidik, *Times of India* 2016): he turned after being told Sukhdev had already confessed. "I could not risk going down with people I no longer respected." He also wrote: "I hope by the time I die, I would have been fully forgotten."

- **Phanindra Nath Ghosh** (P.W. 3).
  - **Role:** a member of the HSRA Central Committee and its organiser for Bihar (Gupta).
  - **Arrest and pardon:** arrested after the Maulania dacoity in Champaran (June 1929). Gupta: "He was recently married and became an approver."
  - **What he gave away:** testimony on the Bihar, Calcutta, UP and Delhi phases of the conspiracy (Noorani); he also testified in later Bihar cases.
  - **Afterwards:** Bhagwan Das wounded him during the Bhusawal case. He was killed by Baikunth Shukla at Bettiah on 9 Nov 1932, and Shukla was hanged at Gaya on 14 May 1934 (Gupta; Wikipedia gives the dates, flagged "citation needed").

- **Man Mohan Banerji** (P.W. 4): testified on the Bihar and Calcutta phases. **Lalit Kumar Mukerji** (P.W. 6): an Allahabad science student who helped teach bomb-making at Agra; testified on Allahabad and Agra (Noorani). What happened to either afterwards: not found.

- **Retracted:** Ram Saran Das "roundly accused the police … of altering his statement". Brahm Dutt also went back on his statement. Mahabir Singh, Gaya Prasad and Prem Dutt retracted confessions (Noorani).

- **The comrades' verdict:** Ajoy Ghosh: "unable to stand police torture, no less than seven — two of them members of our Central Committee — had turned approvers" (quoted in Noorani).

### Police, prosecutors, judges, witnesses

- **J.A. Scott, SSP Lahore:** the HSRA's intended target, held responsible for the lathi charge (Sanyal: "held responsible for the Police beating").
- **Khan Bahadur Abdul Aziz, SP:** led the Saunders and bomb-factory investigation. He arranged the Rs 1,000 reward for informer Babu Singh and ran the blacksmith lead. The Kashmir Buildings landlord admitted Abdul Aziz "was his father's friend". **Witnesses brought in by Abdul Aziz:**
  - Syed Buddhe Shah, a search witness: "I am a truthful witness" (laughter in court).
  - Another search witness claimed Sukhdev threatened to shoot "the Khan Bahadur".
- **IGP Stead:** matched the shells to the Assembly bombs.
- **Sergeant Terry:** arrested Bhagat Singh. The accused called his pistol-seizure evidence "a deliberate falsehood".
- **Robert Churchill, London gun expert (P.W. 31):** tied the cartridge case to Ex. P. 480.
- **Crown counsel C.H. Carden-Noad:** told the defence not to "get their instructions from the gutter". He argued against the last petition on 23 Mar 1931.
- **Justice Hilton and Justice Coldstream:** Coldstream ordered handcuffing on 12 May 1930. Hilton presided at the sentence.
- **Justice Agha Haider:** dissociated himself in writing from the order and was dropped (Noorani). **A counter-example.**
- **Allahabad, 27 Feb 1931:** Nott-Bower and a police party under DSP Vishweshwar Singh (spelled "Bisheshar Singh" in Gupta) cornered Azad, who had a Rs 10,000 reward on his head (Noorani; Gupta).

### Organisations and the press: how each reacted, in their own words

- **Government of India.**
  - Irwin, 29 Apr 1929: "The bomb business in the Assembly may turn out to be a blessing in disguise."
  - Irwin, 1931: "could find no grounds on which I could justify to my conscience commuting the sentence."
  - **Its explanation**, the Ordinance statement: the trial "has tended to bring the administration of justice into contempt".
- **Central Legislative Assembly**, 11 Apr 1929: a unanimous motion records "horror and indignation at the dastardly outrage". On 24 Mar 1931, Diwan Bahadur T. Rangachariar protested the executions and the Nationalists walked out (Noorani).
- **Congress, on the bombing.**
  - Gandhi (*Young India*, 18 Apr 1929): "the bomb throwers have discredited the cause of freedom in whose name they threw the bombs".
  - Motilal Nehru (*Times of India*, 10 Apr 1929): "The choice lies between Gandhi and 'Balraj'."
  - Jawaharlal Nehru: "Were the lathi blows that fell on Lala Lajpat Rai an attempt to reason with him?" (Maclean)
- **Congress, on the hanging.**
  - Gandhi, Karachi: "There can be therefore no excuse for suspicion that I did not want to save Bhagat Singh. But I want you also to realize Bhagat Singh's error."
  - Nehru, 24 Mar 1931: "I have remained absolutely silent during their last days, lest a word of mine may injure their prospect of commutation."
  - The Karachi resolution, drafted by Gandhi: "admiration of the bravery and sacrifice … an act of wanton vengeance".
  - Gandhi's explanation of his conduct: commutation "could not be so made [a condition]. And to threaten withdrawal would be a breach of faith."
  - Young protesters met Gandhi at Malir station shouting "Down with Gandhism". Gandhi: "a legitimate expression of their anger".
- **Hindu Mahasabha:** I found no formal resolution. Madan Mohan Malaviya, a leading figure in both the Mahasabha and Congress, filed a mercy appeal (reported as 14 Feb 1931). He backed Jinnah's Assembly speech ("Sir, cannot we go on for another 15 minutes?") and seconded the Karachi resolution (*Mainstream*).
- **Muslim League:** I found no formal resolution. M.A. Jinnah, speaking in the Assembly on 12 and 14 Sep 1929 against trying hunger strikers in their absence: "The man who goes on hunger strike has a soul. He is moved by that soul and he believes in the justice of his cause." Maulana Zafar Ali Khan moved the unanimous resolution at Lahore's protest meeting of 23 Mar 1931 and led prayers on 24 Mar (Noorani).
- **Communists:** *Workers' Weekly* (13 Nov 1930), as quoted by Organiser (a hostile source), called the HSRA's individual terrorism "a psychology of revenge and not revolution". Later, CPI general secretary Ajoy Ghosh, an acquitted co-accused, wrote the admiring 1945 pamphlet. Bhagat Singh himself had worked on Sohan Singh Josh's *Kirti*.
- **Akali and Sikh leaders:** Sardar Mangal Singh and Sardar Sardul Singh Caveeshar spoke at the Lahore protest meeting on 23 Mar 1931 (Noorani). Bhai Randhir Singh's account of the jail meeting: see §10.
- **The press.**
  - The Tribune first called the pair "lunatics": the accused complained of it on 6 Jun 1929. It later printed the whole trial and a six-part editorial against the case (K.C. Yadav).
  - The Civil and Military Gazette was pro-government (Yadav).
  - The Modern Review mocked "Long Live Revolution" (Dec 1929).
  - Labour leader Dewan Chaman Lal called the bombing a "dastardly outrage"; the accused replied by calling him "pseudo-socialist".
  - The Hindustan Times rushed out an evening edition with the leaflet; CID reports suspected its staff of sympathy (Chaman Lal).
