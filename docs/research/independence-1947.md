# Dossier: The weeks around 15 August 1947

Researcher's note on method. Every fact below was checked against a primary or near-primary text where possible. The main ones are: the Constituent Assembly Debates for 14 August 1947, read in full; Mansergh's *Transfer of Power* Vol. XII, where I searched the full text and read the key documents; V.P. Menon's two books (relevant chapters); Campbell-Johnson's diary, *Mission with Mountbatten* (June to August 1947 chapters); *Collected Works of Mahatma Gandhi* Vol. 89 (9 to 26 August); and the Pakistan Constituent Assembly debates for 10 to 14 August 1947. *Freedom at Midnight* (FaM) is quoted only where it is the sole source, and that is always flagged. Confidence labels: **solid** means primary record; **disputed** means a range or conflict is given; **single-source** means only one account exists.

---

## 1. Summary

On Tuesday 3 June 1947 the British announced that India would be partitioned. They then delivered two Dominions in 73 days. A British Act fixed 15 August as the "appointed day". Roughly 560 princely states had to choose a side; Menon himself gives 562 in one place and "something like 565" in another, so give a range. A London lawyer named Cyril Radcliffe had never been to India before he landed on 8 July. He was given about five weeks to draw lines through Punjab and Bengal. He signed the Punjab and Bengal awards on 12 August and the Sylhet award on 13 August. The Viceroy deliberately kept all of them sealed until after the celebrations, and they were published on Sunday 17 August. So for roughly 60 hours millions of people celebrated freedom without knowing which country they lived in. Officials governed on a provisional "notional" district map, and flags went up in towns that were about to be handed to the other side. Delhi had a midnight session, conches, a rainbow, and crowds ten times larger than planned. Lahore and Amritsar were burning. Gandhi was in Calcutta, fasting and spinning in a borrowed house with his former opponent H.S. Suhrawardy. That gap between joy and uncertainty is what makes the moment dramatic. So does the amount of moving paperwork: telegrams, leaked maps, rumours. It suits a game about telling true messages from forged ones.

## 2. Timeline

### Wider arc (brief)
- **Tue 3 Jun:** Attlee announces the plan in the Commons. That evening Mountbatten, Nehru, Jinnah and Baldev Singh broadcast on All India Radio (AIR). Nehru ends with "Jai Hind", Jinnah with "Pakistan Zindabad". (Campbell-Johnson, CJ)
- **Wed 4 Jun:** At his press conference Mountbatten gives "the first informal indication that 15th August would be the likely date". (CJ, the press attaché who organised it)
- **20 Jun / 23 Jun:** The Bengal and Punjab legislatures vote for partition of their provinces.
- **27 Jun:** The Partition Council agrees that Radcliffe will chair both Boundary Commissions. Jinnah proposed him. (Viceroy's Personal Report No. 17; CJ)
- **30 Jun:** The Commissions are constituted by the Governor-General's announcement, Ref. No. D50/7/47R. (Radcliffe's awards, Mansergh XII)
- **Fri 4 Jul:** The Indian Independence Bill is introduced in the Commons. **Sat 5 Jul:** the States Department is inaugurated with Patel's statement. Menon took charge on 5 July. (Menon)
- **Tue 8 Jul:** Radcliffe arrives in Delhi. **Thu 10 Jul:** he leaves for Calcutta and Lahore. (Mansergh XII intro)
- **15/16 Jul:** The Bill passes the Commons, then the Lords, with no amendments. **Fri 18 Jul:** Royal Assent. (Menon; Mansergh)
- **16 to 24 Jul:** Bengal Commission public sittings in Calcutta, no sitting Sunday 20th. Radcliffe did not attend them in person. **21 to 31 Jul:** Punjab sittings in Lahore, no sitting Sunday 27th. (Radcliffe's own awards)
- **Tue 22 Jul:** The Constituent Assembly adopts the flag. The Partition Council pledges to accept the awards "whatever these may be". **23 Jul:** Radcliffe writes: "I think that I can promise the 12th."
- **Fri 25 Jul:** Mountbatten addresses the Chamber of Princes. On the same day C.P. Ramaswami Aiyar, Dewan of Travancore, is attacked in Trivandrum. **Wed 30 Jul:** the Maharaja of Travancore accepts accession.
- **Fri 1 Aug:** The Punjab Boundary Force is set up under Maj-Gen T.W. Rees: "about 50,000 officers and men" (Menon). Mountbatten put it at "55 thousand soldiers" on 26 August.

## 3. Persona options

**A. Cipher and telegraph clerk, Government House (Viceroy's House), New Delhi, 7 to 17 August.** Every MOST IMMEDIATE telegram passes this desk: Lahore, Karachi, Calcutta, Lucknow, the India Office. The case file has real formats to check against: numbering (No. 3408-S from Delhi, No. 7-K from Karachi), "Begins … Ends.", "Received:" stamps, and "[group corrupt]" for garbled cipher groups. The pressure ramps up naturally, from train-mine reports to the award crisis telegram to midnight. *Why it works:* the source documents are almost all telegrams, and each one can be checked on sender, number, time, place and spelling.

**B. Junior assistant, States Department (V.P. Menon's office), 5 July to 18 August.** Instruments of Accession, telegrams and letters arrive from rulers. Real tells are available: Travancore's acceptance by telegram on 30 July; Bhopal asking that his signature be kept secret for ten days; Indore's papers arriving "in an ordinary postal envelope" with no covering letter; Junagadh's accession reaching Delhi through the newspapers of 17 August. *Why it works:* there is a finite list of states, rulers, Dewans and dates, which suits a forger who swaps a ruler's title or a Dewan's name.

**C. Clerk-typist, Boundary Commission secretariat, Comptroller's House, Viceregal estate, 8 July to 17 August.** This clerk types schedules in tahsils, zails and villages and handles the Bikaner and Bahawalpur representations. They see the 8 August leak and the rumours. *Why it works:* it carries the highest dramatic irony, because the player knows a line that the country does not. Forgeries can move a tahsil (Zira or Ferozepore), a river (Ujh or Ravi) or a date (11th or 12th). Variant: a sub-editor in the AIR newsroom who must refuse to broadcast unconfirmed "award" rumours. The Commander-in-Chief's report noted that "the wildest rumours are current".

## 4. Case-file facts (persona-relevant)

1. **Appointed day: 15 August 1947, a Friday.** The Indian Independence Act 1947 received Royal Assent on **Friday 18 July**. It was introduced 4 July and passed the Commons on 15 July and the Lords on 16 July. — Menon, *Transfer of Power*; Mansergh XII. **Solid.**
2. **Notional districts.** The Act's First Schedule put these Bengal districts provisionally in East Bengal: Chittagong, Noakhali, Tippera; Bakarganj, Dacca, Faridpur, Mymensingh; **Jessore, Murshidabad, Nadia**; Bogra, Dinajpur, **Malda**, Pabna, Rajshahi, Rangpur. The Second Schedule put these in West Punjab: Gujranwala, **Gurdaspur**, Lahore, Sheikhupura, Sialkot; Attock, Gujrat, Jhelum, Mianwali, Rawalpindi, Shahpur; Dera Ghazi Khan, Jhang, Lyallpur, Montgomery, Multan, Muzaffargarh. Khulna and the Chittagong Hill Tracts were *not* listed for East Bengal. — Act text in Menon, appendix. **Solid.**
3. **Commission members.** Punjab: Justices **Din Muhammad, Muhammad Munir, Mehr Chand Mahajan, Teja Singh**. Bengal: Justices **Bijan Kumar Mukherjea, C.C. Biswas, Abu Saleh Mohamed Akram, S.A. Rahman**. Chairman of both: Sir Cyril Radcliffe. His private secretary: **Christopher Beaumont**. — Awards, Mansergh XII. **Solid.**
4. **Terms of reference:** "demarcate the boundaries of the two parts of the Punjab [Bengal] on the basis of ascertaining the contiguous majority areas of Muslims and non-Muslims. In doing so, it will also take into account other factors." — Awards. **Solid.**
5. **Radcliffe arrived in Delhi on 8 July** and lived and worked at the **Comptroller's House** on the Viceregal estate. — Mansergh XII; CJ. **Solid.**
6. **Award dates.** Punjab and Bengal: **12 August**. Sylhet: **13 August**. Radcliffe signed the Punjab map: "Attached to my Report d. 12 August 1947 for reference." — Mansergh XII, Appendices to No. 488. **Solid.** (Menon's memoir says "ready … only on 13 August" and copies handed over on the 17th. The documents show the 16th. **Treat Menon as wrong by a day.**)
7. **Handover and publication.** Ministers received copies after the Joint Defence Council on the morning of **Sat 16 August**. The meeting was at **5.00 pm** at Government House. The awards were published as a **Gazette Extraordinary** on **Sun 17 August**. — Minutes, Mansergh XII; Chester. **Solid.**
8. **Outcome in Punjab.** East Punjab got about **38% of the area and 45% of the population**; West Punjab got about 62% and 55%. Of Gurdaspur, only the **Shakargarh tahsil** went to Pakistan. — Menon; Chester; award schedule. **Solid.** (Chester's article reverses the area figures. Use Menon's.)
9. **Outcome in Bengal.** West Bengal got about **36% of the area and 35% of the population**. Murshidabad went to India. Khulna went to East Bengal, except areas west of the Mathabhanga. The Chittagong Hill Tracts went to East Bengal. — Menon; Banglapedia. **Solid.**
10. **The leaked forecast.** Abell to Abbott, 8 Aug, TOP SECRET. Jenkins later described the enclosed line as "a sharp salient in the Ferozepore District … enclosed the whole of the Ferozepore and Zira Tahsils". The final award kept both in India. — Mansergh XII No. 377 and note. **Solid.**
11. **Chittagong Hill Tracts population:** under a quarter of a million (Mountbatten), stated as "97% Buddhist and Hindu" by Nehru at the 16 Aug meeting. — Mansergh XII. **Solid**, with the wording attributed.
12. **Midnight session.** It opened at **"Eleven P. M."** on 14 Aug in the **Constitution Hall, New Delhi** (the Council House), with **Dr Rajendra Prasad**, President of the Constituent Assembly, in the chair. — CAD 14 Aug. **Solid.**
13. **Running order:** Vande Mataram (first verse, by **Sucheta Kripalani**); Prasad's address in Hindustani, then English; **two minutes' silence**; Nehru's motion, in Hindustani and then English; seconded by **Chaudhri Khaliquzzaman**; speech by **S. Radhakrishnan**; **H.V. Kamath** drops his amendments "since the zero hour is fast approaching"; the pledge at midnight; intimation to the Viceroy; **Hansa Mehta** presents the flag "on behalf of the women of India", "in the absense of Shrimati Sarojini Naidu"; **Sare Jahan se Achcha** (first lines) and **Jana Gana Mana** (first verse) by Sucheta Kripalani; adjournment "till Ten of the Clock on Friday, the 15th August 1947". — CAD. **Solid.**
14. **The pledge moment.** Prasad: "It is just half a minute to 12. I am expecting the clock to strike 12." The pledge was read sentence by sentence, Hindustani first, then English. — CAD. **Solid.** Conches were blown at midnight. The CAD does not record this, but it appears in a contemporary *Tribune* report ("As the clock chimed twelve, conches blew"), in UK High Commissioner Shone's despatch ("whistles, hooters and conch shells"), and in the *New York Times* (Trumbull: "an unidentified member"). **Solid that conches sounded; who blew them is unknown.**
15. **Flag (adopted Tue 22 July):** "horizontal tricolour of deep Saffron (Kesari), white and dark green in equal proportion … a Wheel in navy blue to represent the Charkha … the Wheel (Chakra) which appears on the abacus of the Sarnath Lion Capital of Asoka … ratio … ordinarily 2:3." — CAD 22 Jul. **Solid.**
16. **Pakistan.** The Karachi Assembly sat at **9.10 am, Thu 14 Aug**. Mountbatten said: "I am speaking to you today as your Viceroy. Tomorrow the Government of the new Dominion of Pakistan will rest in your hands". Jinnah was sworn in as Governor-General on **15 Aug**. The Pakistan flag resolution was moved on **11 Aug**: "dark green … white vertical bar at the mast … white crescent … five pointed white heraldic star". — Pakistan CA Debates; Menon. **Solid.**
17. **Delhi, 15 Aug.** 8.30 am Durbar Hall: "God Save the King" followed by Jana Gana Mana. Council House crowd "over a quarter of a million", flag raised, **31-gun salute**. Princes Park was planned for about 30,000 and drew "nearer three hundred thousand". — CJ. **Solid as eyewitness estimates.**
18. **Red Fort flag: Sat 16 Aug, "very early".** Nehru addressed "some half a million". — CJ. **Solid.**
19. **States Department.** Minister: **Sardar Patel**. Secretary: **V.P. Menon**, who took charge on 5 July. By 15 Aug, all states contiguous to India had signed the Instrument of Accession and Standstill Agreement **except Junagadh and two small Kathiawar states**. Hyderabad was being negotiated separately, and the Ministry "had made no approach" to Kashmir. — Menon, *Integration*. **Solid.**
20. **Junagadh** announced accession to Pakistan by communiqué on **15 Aug**. India first learned of it from the newspapers of **17 Aug**. — Menon. **Solid.**
21. **Gandhi.** Reached Calcutta **9 Aug**. Moved with Suhrawardy into a house in **Beliaghata** on the evening of **Wed 13 Aug**. Spent 15 Aug in "prayer and fasting and spinning", fasting on fruit juice by his own description. — CWMG 89. **Solid.**
22. **Punjab Boundary Force:** set up **1 Aug** under **Maj-Gen T.W. Rees**, "about 50,000" (Menon). — **Solid.**
23. **Money.** Pakistan's share of undivided assets and liabilities was **17.5%** (widely documented). India transferred **Rs 20 crore** as working cash before agreement (Menon). On 13 Aug the allocation of cash balances was still unsettled and headed for the Arbitral Tribunal (Mansergh No. 462). The later agreed figure was **Rs 75 crore**. Currency: **1 rupee = 16 annas = 64 pice = 192 pies**. Inland letter **1½ annas**, postcard **½ anna (6 pies)** at 15 Aug 1947 (philatelic rate tables). **Solid / moderate.**
24. **Officials, all spelled as in the records.** Sir Evan Jenkins (Governor, Punjab); Sir Frederick Burrows (Governor, Bengal); Sir Francis Mudie (West Punjab designate); Sir Chandulal Trivedi (East Punjab designate); C. Rajagopalachari (West Bengal); Sir Frederick Bourne (East Bengal); Lord Ismay; Sir Eric Miéville; Field Marshal Sir Claude Auchinleck; Sir Patrick Spens (Arbitral Tribunal). Mountbatten is styled **Viscount** Mountbatten of Burma in August 1947; the earldom was created 28 Oct 1947. — Mansergh; CJ. **Solid.**
25. **Time zone.** Delhi and Karachi were on the same clock, UTC+5:30, in 1947. Pakistan did not move to UTC+5 until 1951. London (BST) was 4½ hours behind, so London "Received" times look *earlier* than sending times. Example: "KARACHI, 13 August 1947, 8.30 pm … Received: 13 August, 5.45 pm". — Mansergh telegram headers; time-zone histories. **Solid / moderate.** Calcutta and Bombay local times reportedly lingered in civil use; that claim is commonly stated but I have not verified it.
26. **Displacement.** Census-based estimate: **14.5 million inflows, 17.9 million outflows, about 3.4 million "missing"** (Bharadwaj, Khwaja and Mian, EPW 2008). Butalia: about 12 million displaced, and 673 refugee trains carried about 2.8 million people between August and November 1947. Deaths: estimates run from **200,000 (Penderel Moon) to 2 million**, and many historians use about 1 million. **Disputed; always give the range.**

## 5. Real messages (seed "genuine" telegrams)

All verbatim from the cited source.

1. **Abell to Abbott, 8 Aug 1947, TOP SECRET** (Mansergh XII No. 377): "I enclose a map showing roughly the boundary which Sir Cyril Radcliffe proposes to demarcate in his award, and a note by Christopher Beaumont describing it. There will not be any great changes from this boundary, but it will have to be accurately defined with reference to village and zail boundaries in Lahore district. The award itself is expected within the next 48 hours…"
2. **Abbott to Abell, telegram, IMMEDIATE, 9 Aug 1947, 12.00 pm, CONFIDENTIAL, Received 2.00 pm, No. 221-S** (No. 391): "Your letter of August 8th. Governor is taking law and order action on preliminary information given. He trusts final version will be very precise and will be related as far as possible to existing administrative units and boundaries… he would like the document in final form 24 hours before its release…"
3. **Abell to Governor's Secretary, Sind, IMMEDIATE, 10 Aug 1947, 11.00 pm, No. 3312-S** (No. 418): "Begins. At 2100 hours on August 9th a Pakistan special train ran over a mine on the track 15 miles west of Bhatinda in East Punjab. The mine exploded, blowing 11 feet out of the track and derailing the engine and six coaches. Casualties 1 women and 1 child killed, 10 persons slightly injured… Ends." (The original reads "1 women".)
4. **Radcliffe to Mountbatten, 23 July 1947**: "I do not think that I could manage the 10th: but I think that I can promise the 12th, and I will do the earlier date if I possibly can." The original underlines "think".
5. **Mountbatten to Listowel, IMMEDIATE, NEW DELHI, 14 Aug 1947, 10.15 pm, Received 15 Aug 2.30 am, No. 3408-S**: "A crisis has arisen over awards of the Boundary Commissions… I personally have scrupulously avoided all connection with Boundary Commissions… (a) I have decided not to publish awards myself; (b) I have told Governors concerned that new Provinces must carry on on notional partition boundaries on 15th…"
6. **Mountbatten to Listowel, MOST IMMEDIATE, KARACHI, 13 Aug, 8.30 pm, No. 7-K**: "Your 10491 of 12th August. Assets and liabilities. I fear it is quite out of the question for you to decide the allocation of cash balances…"
7. **Sir F. Wylie to Mountbatten, IMMEDIATE, LUCKNOW, 14 Aug 1947, 2.45 pm, UNNUMBERED, Received in India Office 12.10 pm**: "The Union Jack on Lucknow Residency was lowered at 20 hours on 13 August." Mansergh notes that Wylie "acted a day early". The plan had been sundown on 14 August.
8. **Punjab award, para 12** (signed CYRIL RADCLIFFE): "I am conscious too that the award cannot go far towards satisfying sentiments and aspirations deeply held on either side…"
9. **Maharaja of Travancore to Mountbatten, PALACE, TRIVANDRUM, 30 July 1947**: "I have decided, though not without hesitation, to take this step of accession to the Dominion of India…" Signed RAMA VARMA.
10. **Junagadh communiqué, 15 Aug**: "…the Government of the State has decided to accede to Pakistan and hereby announces its decision to that effect." (Menon)
11. **Nehru, 14 Aug, CAD transcript**: "Long years ago we made a tryst with destiny, and now the time comes when we shall redeem our pledge, not wholly or in full measure, but very substantially. At the stroke of the midnight hour, when the world sleeps, India will awake to life and freedom. A moment comes, which comes but rarely in history, when we step out from the old to the new, when an age ends, and when the soul of a nation, long suppressed, finds utterance."
12. **The pledge** (as administered): "At this solemn moment when the people of India, through suffering and sacrifice, have secured freedom, I………. a member of the Constituent Assembly of India, do dedicate myself in all humility to the service of India and her people to the end that this ancient land attain her rightful and honoured place in the world and make her full and willing contribution to the promotion of world peace and the welfare [of] mankind."
13. **Assembly to Viceroy, intimation moved after midnight**: "(1) the Constituent Assembly of India has assumed power for the governance of India, and (2) … endorsed the recommendation that Lord Mountbatten be Governor-General of India from the 15th August 1947."
14. **At Government House after midnight** (CJ). Mountbatten: "I am proud of the honour, and I will do my best to carry out your advice in a constitutional manner." Nehru: "May I submit to you the portfolios of the new Cabinet?" The envelope turned out to be empty.
15. **King's message to Pakistan, read 14 Aug**: "I send you my greetings and warmest wishes on this great occasion when the new Dominion of Pakistan is about to take its place in the British Commonwealth of Nations…"
16. **Jinnah, 11 Aug**: "You are free; you are free to go to your temples, you are free to go to your mosques or to any other place of worship in this State of Pakistan."
17. **Gandhi to Agatha Harrison, Beliaghata, 15 Aug**: "This letter I am dictating whilst I am spinning. You know, my way of celebrating great events, such as today's, is to thank God for it and, therefore, to pray. This prayer must be accompanied by a fast, if the taking of fruit juices may be so described."
18. **Gandhi, prayer meeting, 14 Aug** (reported): he invited everyone "to have twenty-four hours' fast and prayer… and pass it in spinning as much as possible."
19. **Mountbatten to Gandhi, 26 Aug**: "In the Punjab we have 55 thousand soldiers and large-scale rioting on our hands. In Bengal our forces consist of one man, and there is no rioting… may I be allowed to pay my tribute to the One-man Boundary Force, not forgetting his Second in Command, Mr. Suhrawardy."
20. **Mountbatten's V.J.-Day broadcast**, recorded 8 Aug and transmitted 15 Aug via AIR and the BBC to the US: "…let us remember August 15th—V.J. Day—not only as the celebration of a victory, but also as the fulfilment of a pledge."
21. **Press on the award** (via Butalia): *Amrita Bazar Patrika*, "departing kick of British imperialism at both the Hindus and Muslims"; *Dawn*, "territorial murder". This is a balanced pair.
22. **Garbled cipher, Jenkins situation telegram**: "Ludhiana reports village raid with 1 Muslim killed and 2 [group corrupt] killed." Use only the format, not the casualty content.

## 6. Real deception, leaks and rumour

- **The Ferozepore "salient" leak.** Abell's 8 Aug letter and map went to Lahore. Then, Jenkins wrote in April 1948, "About the 10th or 11th August … I received a secraphone message from Viceroy's House containing the words 'Eliminate Salient'." The final award gave Ferozepore and Zira to India. Mountbatten in 1948: "Abell sent the letter concerned without my knowledge. It may be hard to convince people that that was so. It will look to have been an odd procedure." Campbell-Johnson's defence: a "strictly provisional forecast … wrong to the extent of two tehsils and two days." Beaumont in 1992 and Pakistani accounts allege that Mountbatten pressed Radcliffe. Chester (2002) judges it "may well" have happened. *Documented both ways; present as an open question.*
- **Indian-side lobbying is also documented.** On 9 Aug Nehru forwarded A.N. Khosla's note to Mountbatten, suggesting it "might be sent on to Sir Cyril Radcliffe". The note said "It is gathered that at the luncheon at Simla Sir Cyril Radcliffe suggested…" and warned it "will be disastrous" if Ferozepore and Zira went to Pakistan. Bikaner and Bahawalpur made representations, which Radcliffe acknowledged in para 8 of his award.
- **Pakistan-side early warning.** On 11 Aug Liaquat sent a verbal message, via Pakistan's Cabinet Secretary, that Gurdaspur "or a large portion of it" had gone to East Punjab, and that if this was "a political decision and not a judicial one" it would be "a breach of faith". The award had not yet been submitted. (Mansergh No. 489)
- **Deliberate withholding.** Mountbatten wrote that he "deliberately refrained from reading" the Bengal award. Menon warned that Congress would "blow up" over the Chittagong Hill Tracts and might boycott the 15 August events, so publication waited until after the 15th (Report No. 17).
- **Rumour as a military problem.** Auchinleck's note around 15 Aug: "lacking an announcement, the wildest rumours are current, and are being spread by mischief makers."
- **Flags in the wrong places (notional period).** Under the First Schedule, Murshidabad, Malda and Nadia were notionally East Bengal, and Khulna and the CHT notionally West Bengal. Local and press accounts describe Pakistan flags over Murshidabad (the Hazarduari palace) and parts of Nadia on 15 August, and the tricolour in the CHT capital, Rangamati. The award reversed each of these. Nadia towns commemorate 18 August. Pakistani troops took down the Rangamati flag on 17 or 21 Aug; the accounts conflict. *Solid in outline; exact dates vary by source.*
- **The Karachi bomb plot.** There were reports of a plot to bomb Jinnah's procession on 14 Aug. Mountbatten rode with him and "nothing happened" (Menon). Jinnah: "Thank God I have brought you back alive." (CJ)
- **The 3 June mishearing.** Jinnah's clipped "Pakistan Zindabad" made "some startled listeners" think he had said "Pakistan's in the bag" (CJ). The Dominion Status plan had already leaked to the Indian press that morning (CJ).
- **Secret accessions.** Bhopal signed "with the stipulation that his signature should be kept secret for ten days after the transfer of power" (Menon).
- **Censorship (disputed).** Zamir Niazi (*The Press in Chains*, 1986) claims officials tried to keep Jinnah's 11 August speech out of the papers. Others dispute it. *Label it a claim.*
- **Destroyed evidence.** Radcliffe confirmed in a letter that "he had destroyed his own notes and drafts" (Mansergh XII note). The original award maps "have not been traced".

## 8. Myths versus the record

- **"Astrologers chose midnight."** This comes from FaM, which tells a dramatic story of a "Swamin Madamanand" writing to Mountbatten and claims "a congeries of astrologers" advised a 14 August solution. The record shows something else. The Act fixed 15 Aug as the appointed day, and the Assembly met at 11 pm so it could take power "after the last stroke of midnight" (CAD). The famous Viceregal "astrology" minute is real, but it was dated **7 Aug** and was about Pakwasa's travel plans, not the date of independence (CJ). FaM moves that minute to just after the June press conference. *No documentary support found for astrologers setting the time.*
- **"Mountbatten announced 'The final Transfer of Power … will take place on 15 August 1947' on 4 June."** Campbell-Johnson, who ran the event, records only "the first informal indication that 15th August would be the likely date". The ringing line is FaM's.
- **"Nehru gave 'Tryst with Destiny' at the Red Fort on 15 Aug."** He gave it in the Constituent Assembly, Council House, late on 14 Aug. The Red Fort flag went up on 16 Aug.
- **"The Radcliffe Line was announced on Independence Day."** It was published on 17 Aug, and the leaders saw it on the 16th.
- **"Pakistan became independent a day before India."** Both Dominions came into being on 15 Aug under the Act. Mountbatten addressed Karachi on the 14th because he could not be in two capitals at midnight. Pakistan's first commemorative stamps (July 1948) read "15 August 1947". Pakistan settled on 14 August later; sources say 1948 or 1949.
- **"Gandhi boycotted independence."** He did not boycott it. He marked it with "prayer and fasting and spinning", wrote letters, held a prayer meeting, and urged people everywhere to fast and spin. N.K. Bose records him telling Communist visitors: "I can't afford to take part in this rejoicing, which is a sorry affair."
- **"Jodhpur pulled a pen-pistol on Menon."** Menon's own account says "a revolver", and his reply was "Don't indulge in juvenile theatricals." The pen-pistol and its afterlife in the Magic Circle museum come from FaM.
- **Nehru and Edwina Mountbatten.** A close friendship is documented in their letters. Their daughter Pamela Hicks, having read the letters, calls it intense but not physical. No document shows Edwina shaping the boundary or the states policy.
- **"Radcliffe had never been to India."** True; Chester says so. But "he drew it alone with no input" is false. Commission judges, counsel, memoranda and lobbying are all on record.
- **The conch.** It is real, but nobody blew it "from the gallery" as FaM says. Contemporary reports name no one.

## 9. Sensory world

- **Sound.** The AIR signature tune: tanpura drone, then a slow violin and viola melody (Raga Shivaranjini), commonly credited to Walter Kaufmann, 1936. Conches, whistles and hooters at midnight (Shone). Chants of "Jai Hind", "Mahatma Gandhi ki jai", and on 15 Aug even "Mountbatten ki jai" and "Pandit Mountbatten" (CJ). In Calcutta, "Hindu-Muslims! Be one" and "Long Live Hindustan and Pakistan" from mixed crowds (CWMG). Trumpets and scarlet-and-gold at the Durbar Hall; a photographer's flash-bulb going off like a bomb; "God Save the King" followed by Jana Gana Mana; a 31-gun salute; the BBC van at Princes Park (Wynford Vaughan Thomas). In Karachi, "a band of bearded warriors in kilts". The telegraph sounder, ceiling fans, a cyclostyle machine, and the Viceroy's tear-off calendar being torn.
- **Weather and light.** Monsoon. Light rain and a rainbow at the Princes Park flag-raising on 15 Aug evening. Rain all through the Bombay parade on 17 Aug. Floods in Chittagong (Gandhi's prayer speech). From the plane on 14 Aug, "large fires, beacons of ill-omen" over Punjab. Over Lahore, "a thick pall of smoke" (Auchinleck). Fairy lights in the Mughal gardens; arc-lamps in Karachi.
- **On the desk (Persona A or C).** Cipher pads; telegram forms stamped IMMEDIATE, MOST IMMEDIATE, TOP SECRET; carbon flimsies; a secraphone handset; the Gazette of India Extraordinary; 1941 Census district tables; quarter-inch survey sheets with tahsil and zail lines; an inkstand, blotter and pins; a Remington-style typewriter, the kind actually fought over in Bengal (§12); the tear-off calendar reading "X Days left to prepare for the Transfer of Power".
- **Scripts and print.** English typescript; Hindustani in Devanagari and in Urdu (Nastaliq); Bengali; Gurmukhi on Punjab notices. Postage in annas. Indian stamps still showed George VI; the first independence stamp ("Jai Hind", flag, 3½ annas) came on 21 Nov 1947.
- **Dress.** Prasad "in white khadi" (*Tribune*); Jinnah's white achkan and monocle; Lady Mountbatten "in gold lamé"; Body Guard lance pennants; Gandhi's dhoti and charkha in a bare Beliaghata room, with Muslim volunteers cooking.
- **Rooms.** In the Assembly chamber, "the empty panels of the portraits on the walls were covered with bright national flags" (*Tribune*). The report does not say whose portraits had hung there. Traffic jams on Parliament Street before 11 pm. The session lasted about 1¼ hours.

## 10. Sensitivity and range of views

- **Name no villains.** Present the boundary as a documented contest. Congress, Sikh leaders, the Muslim League and princely states all lobbied, and the British controlled both the timing and the secrecy. Each claim in §6 has a counter-claim on record, so show both.
- **Violence.** Use aggregates and official language. Every community suffered, and contemporary reports record aggression by members of each. Never depict a perpetrator community. Never use a casualty number without its range. Avoid train-massacre imagery and avoid gore. Abducted women (Butalia gives a figure of about 75,000) can appear as a fact of loss and recovery work, never as a scene.
- **Range of interpretation, one line each.** Indian nationalist historians: hurried British withdrawal plus League intransigence. Pakistani historians: Mountbatten and Radcliffe favoured India (Gurdaspur, Ferozepore). Ayesha Jalal's revisionist view: Jinnah used Pakistan as a bargaining position and did not want *this* partition. British apologists (Campbell-Johnson): the speed prevented worse. Chester: the process, not the line, caused the damage. Butalia and Yasmin Khan: an ordinary-people view centred on women, refugees and confusion.
- **Gandhi in Calcutta works because it unites.** A Hindu leader and a Muslim League ex-Premier lived together, and Suhrawardy publicly admitted responsibility for 1946.
- **Hindu Mahasabha and RSS, and the Communists:** see §12 for documented stances with each side's own explanation. Keep them out of the core gameplay.
- **Religion:** never as a punchline, never a "tell". Forgeries should fail on names, places, dates, titles and procedure, never on faith.

## 12. Safe targets for wit, with receipts

Scope: gentle fun at imperial haste, red tape, and title-hunters or opportunists of every stripe. Never at victims, religions or communities.

**A. Radcliffe's five-week line.**
- Timeline: landed 8 July, his first time in India (Chester). Awards dated 12 August, which is **35 days**.
- On 23 July he wrote: "I think that I can promise the 12th." He delivered exactly on the 12th.
- His own award admits he "did not attend the public sittings in person" of the Bengal Commission, because Punjab's were running at the same time.
- Later, to Kuldip Nayar in 1971: "I had no alternative; the time at my disposal was so short that I could not do a better job. Given the same period I would do the same thing. However, if I had two to three years, I might have improved on what I did."
- As quoted by Butalia from a later interview: leaders "told me that they wanted a line before or on 15th August. So I drew them a line."
- Letter to his nephew, as quoted by Butalia: "there will be roughly 80 million people with a grievance who will begin looking for me. I do not want them to find me."
- Auden, "Partition" (1966), safe lines: "Unbiased at least he was when he arrived on his mission, / Having never set eyes on this land he was called to partition"; "The maps at his disposal were out of date / And the Census Returns almost certainly incorrect". Poetic licence to flag: Auden says "seven weeks" (it was about five) and "sailed" home. Avoid his "incompatible gods" line, which is religion-adjacent. The poem is in copyright, so quote only short lines.
- Fee: "refused his fee (£2,000 / Rs 40,000)" is widely repeated, but I found no primary proof. Don't use it.

**B. Mountbatten's date.**
- Contemporary record: 4 June, "first informal indication that 15th August would be the likely date" (CJ).
- His own framing, broadcast to America on 15 August: "let us remember August 15th—V.J. Day—not only as the celebration of a victory, but also as the fulfilment of a pledge."
- Later recollection, to Collins and Lapierre: "I was determined to show I was the master of the whole event." This is attributed to Mountbatten in FaM, so cite it as his later recollection.
- The prop: his tear-off calendar for staff and ministers, "X Days left to prepare for the Transfer of Power" (CJ; Menon).
- Self-mockery on record: after Pakwasa refused to travel on astrological grounds, Mountbatten noted "a complete lack on his staff of high-level advisers on astrology. This would be remedied forthwith." He appointed his Press Attaché "honorary … Astrologer to the Governor-General" (CJ, 7 Aug).
- His farewell toast: his predecessors had been "on a stationary bicycle"; he had been told to start pedalling (CJ).

**C. Red tape in the division of assets.**
- **Documented.** Calcutta, 30 July 1947, Viceroy's 24th Miscellaneous Meeting: a "deadlock in connection with typewriters"; the Centre had decided typewriters be divided "pro rata, based on the genuine requirements"; printing presses, "one in a jail"; new presses for Pakistan ordered from the UK; the Viceroy insisting "All these transactions should be live, and not only paper ones" (Mansergh XII).
- **Documented.** Menon: departments duplicated files and shipped "refrigerators, duplicators and calculating machines to Karachi".
- **Well attested, details single-sourced.** The Governor-General's Bodyguard was split 2:1, and a coin toss gave India the gold state carriage. The call of "Heads" by Govind Singh rests on single sources.
- **Documented.** The Union Jack campaign. Mountbatten tried to get a small Union Jack into the canton of both new flags, and both sides refused. His fallback was to hoist it beside their flags on "recognised days", "if it were not publicised". The Lucknow Residency flag, "never been lowered by day or night", was hauled down a day early (Mansergh).
- **FaM only, uncorroborated; use as "so the story goes" or skip.** *Encyclopaedia Britannica* split alternate volume by volume; dictionaries torn A–K; a Lahore police band fighting over the last trombone; a Food Department inventory including "40 chamber pots".

**D. Princes who bargained.** All from Menon or Mansergh unless noted.
- **Travancore.** Dewan C.P. Ramaswami Aiyar declared it "an independent sovereign State" on 11 June. He held out for an "agreement" instead of accession. He was attacked on 25 July. The Maharaja acceded by telegram and letter on 30 July, "though not without hesitation".
- **Hyderabad.** Made a similar independence announcement on 12 June. On 13 August, the last day of the Raj, the Nizam was still getting London to give his *second son* the title "His Highness"; Patel had "no objection". Hyderabad was given two more months. It signed a Standstill Agreement in November 1947 and was integrated after Indian military action in September 1948.
- **Bhopal.** Skipped the 25 July meeting, saying rulers were "invited like the Oysters to attend the tea party with the Walrus and the Carpenter". He signed on condition of ten days' secrecy, then wrote: "I can be as staunch a friend as I have been an inveterate opponent."
- **Jodhpur.** Menon was told Jinnah handed the Maharaja a signed blank sheet and his fountain pen: "You can fill in all your conditions." After signing for India, the Maharaja levelled a revolver at Menon: "Don't indulge in juvenile theatricals."
- **Indore.** Ignored the invitations, then sent the signed papers "in an ordinary postal envelope".
- **Junagadh.** Menon calls the Nawab's "chief preoccupation … dogs, of which he owned hundreds", including a dog wedding with a state holiday ("I was told"). He announced accession to Pakistan on 15 Aug without telling India. He flew to Karachi in late October with "some of his dogs" and the state's cash and securities. Feeding the dogs he left behind cost Rs 16,000 a month. In the 20 Feb 1948 referendum, 190,870 voted and 91 chose Pakistan. Keep the joke about the ruler, not his faith.
- **Kashmir.** No approach was made to the Maharaja before 15 Aug. He acceded on 26 Oct 1947; some historians argue the 27th.

**E. Documented 1947 stances that embarrassed a group, each with its own explanation (fairness pairs).**
- **The Viceroy's staff.** The Abell leak was "without my knowledge … an odd procedure" (Mountbatten). *Their line:* "wrong to the extent of two tehsils and two days. There is … no more nor less to it than that" (CJ).
- **Congress leaders.** They assured a Chittagong Hill Tracts delegation that it could never go to Pakistan. Menon conceded to Mountbatten that "they had no possible authority". Patel's 13 Aug letter threatened resistance despite the pledge to accept the awards. *Their line (Nehru, 16 Aug):* the Tracts were an "excluded area", 97% non-Muslim, outside the terms of reference, "after consultation with lawyers".
- **Pakistan government / Muslim League.** On 13 Aug the Pakistan cabinet asked Mountbatten to slip emergency Section 93 powers into the adaptation order. His advisers called it "a fraud on Independence Act". Liaquat warned of a "breach of faith" before the award even existed. *Their line:* the Governor-General acts on advice, so it was "neither ultra vires nor improper"; and the Gurdaspur fear rested on information that turned out correct.
- **Mountbatten himself (title-hunting at the Crown's end).** He was pushing Union Jacks onto two nations' flags in the week of their freedom.
- **High heat; recommend not using for jokes.**
  - **RSS:** *Organiser* of 14 Aug 1947 is widely quoted as rejecting the tricolour ("Mystery behind the Bhagwa Dhwaj"). I have not seen the original issue. *Sangh line:* one weekly's opinion during a live debate over symbols, not a call to dishonour; the RSS honours the tricolour today.
  - **CPI:** In Feb 1948 its Calcutta Congress adopted the line behind "Yeh azadi jhooti hai". *Party line:* a Cominform-era analysis that power had passed to a collaborating bourgeoisie. Ranadive was denounced as a "left adventurist" in 1950 and the line was dropped in 1951. In August 1947, under P.C. Joshi, the CPI had supported the Nehru government.
  - Use either only paired with the other, and with the explanation line.
